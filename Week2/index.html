An Introduction to the Journey of Water

Introduce the topic to the kids. Share the opening ayah and how it ties to the week’s topic.

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ الْفُلْكَ تَجْرِي فِي الْبَحْرِ بِنِعْمَتِ اللَّهِ لِيُرِيَكُمْ مِّنْ آيَاتِهِ ۚ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِّكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ ٣١

“Do you not see that ships sail through the sea, by the grace of Allah, to show you some of His wonders? Truly there are signs in this for every steadfast, thankful person.”

This ayah introduces us to another blessing Allah has put in water: its movement. In this lesson, we will explore where water moves and what this movement facilitates. We will learn about the various bodies of water found on earth, how these bodies of water aid in transportation, as well as learn about our local water systems here in DFW. By the end, we hope to be amongst the steadfast and thankful who are able to see how these are all beautiful signs of Allah.

Discovery Den

This is information to share with the kids. What you share and the level of detail will depend on the ages of the kids in your pod. Use your discretion.

Water is a special mercy (Rahmah) from Allah. It doesn’t just sit still; it goes on an epic adventure, flowing and gathering to bring life to forests, deserts, animals, and us!

How does water flow?

  • Rain hits the ground and forms little streams. These streams merge to become mighty rivers, flowing all the way back into the giant oceans. When a freshwater river finally reaches the ocean, something amazing happens. They don’t just instantly mix together like stirring syrup into milk. Allah (SWT) mentions this exact miracle in the Quran. Because ocean water is packed with salt, it is much heavier (denser) than the freshwater from the river. When they bump into each other, the heavier saltwater sinks to the bottom, and the lighter freshwater floats on top.

But how does it gather in our Texas backyard?

  • Rivers (The Big Movers): The main water highway in our area is the Trinity River. It is huge! Rainwater from all over DFW eventually finds its way into the branches of the Trinity River, which flows hundreds of miles south until it reaches the Gulf of Mexico (where it meets the salty ocean!). The branch of Trinity River that flows through our area is called Elm Fork. The Elm Fork starts as a small, natural stream up north in Cooke County. As it flows south toward Dallas, it gathers rainwater from smaller creeks and streams along the way, growing wider and stronger.
  • Streams and Creeks (The Little Helpers): Before water reaches the mighty Trinity River, it travels through smaller streams and creeks right in our neighborhoods, like White Rock Creek or Denton Creek. Think of them as the smaller branches of a big water tree. Rowlett Creek winds through Plano, Garland, and Rowlett before emptying into Lake Ray Hubbard. It is a great local creek to observe seasonal changes in water flow.
  • Lakes (The Giant Gatherers): Did you know that almost all the major lakes in the DFW area are man-made? Lakes like Lake Lewisville, Lake Grapevine, and Lake Ray Hubbard are actually called reservoirs. Because we don’t have a lot of big, natural lakes here, humans built giant walls called dams across the rivers to catch and gather the water. When you turn on your tap at home to get a drink, that water came from one of these lakes!

Does water only gather above ground?

  • Water doesn’t just gather above ground. Sometimes, it soaks deep into the earth and is stored in giant underground “sponges” made of sand, gravel, and rock. These are called aquifers. Just west of us, stretching across the Texas Panhandle and seven other states, is the Ogallala Aquifer. It is one of the largest underground water systems in the entire world!
  • The Ogallala Aquifer is so deep that it holds water that has been trapped underground since the Ice Age—thousands of years ago! Today, farmers pump this water up to water the crops that make the food we eat. Because this underground water is hidden so deep, it refills very, very slowly. Right now, it is shrinking because people are using it faster than nature can refill it. Allah gave us this incredible hidden reservoir as a special provision (Rizq), which reminds us of our job as Khulafa (stewards) of the Earth.

How does water work for transportation and migration?

  • Water as a highway (Transportation): We know that water gives us life, but it also helps us travel! Long before humans built paved highways and airports, Allah (SWT) provided rivers and oceans as the very first roads. Back in the 1800s, before Dallas had big highways or trains, early settlers actually tried to use the Trinity River as a waterway for giant steamboats! They wanted to bring heavy supplies all the way from the Gulf of Mexico up to DFW. While the river ended up being a little too shallow and snaggy for big boats, it is exactly why the city of Dallas was built where it is—right next to a shallow crossing of the Trinity River!
  • Water For The Great Animal Journey (Migration): Water isn’t just a highway for boats; it is a life-saving “gas station” for animals on the move! Every year, millions of birds travel thousands of miles to escape the cold winter in a journey called migration. The sky right above the DFW area is part of a massive, invisible bird highway called the Central Flyway. Birds fly all the way from Canada down to South America.
    Watery Rest Stops: Imagine going on a giant road trip without anywhere to stop, rest, and get a drink! Migrating birds—like ducks, pelicans, and tiny songbirds—rely on our local DFW water bodies. They land in places like the Elm Fork, Lake Lewisville, and the wetlands at the Trinity River Audubon Center to catch fish, drink fresh water, and rest their tired wings before continuing their long flight. Even Monarch butterflies follow our rivers during their migration!

Observing on the Move

Here are some observation prompts to help the kids tune into their environment and engage all of their senses.

For Kids 1–4

Listen to the sound of water. Where do you hear it? Is it a gushing sound or a slow trickle?

Do you feel any water on your skin today? Or dryness?

Are you thirsty? What does that mean for your body?

For Kids 5–8

Water travels through tracks and paths it can find. Where does the surface of water go quiet and flat? That usually means depth. Where does it speed up? And where does it pause, spread out, or disappear into the ground?

Notice what else gathers at the same place — moss, insects, darker soil, roots.

Can you see the boundary where a wet spot becomes dry? Where does the water go?

For Kids 9–14

The 60% water in your body is in conversation with the outside water in the world through temperature, humidity, pressure.

Are you losing water right now (sweating, breathing dry air) or absorbing it (humid, cool)?

Scan the landscape slowly. Where does water choose to stay?

What shape is the ground that receives water— hollow, concave, shadowed, compacted?

Stories for Blooming Hearts

Share a story from our tradition that ties to this week’s topic. Note that creating an engaging storytelling experience is on the storyteller; these are just the basic details. A story told from the heart will reach hearts!

Prophet Musa (AS) as a Baby in the River Nile
  • Prophet Musa AS was born at a time when a very cruel ruler, Fir’awn, was in power. He did not want baby boys to live because he saw a dream and was afraid that one day someone else would take away his power.
  • When Musa AS was born, his mother was so afraid that Fir’awn and his people would find out and she didn’t know what to do. Allah inspired her with the message to nurse him and place him in the river when she fears for him. Allah promised Musa AS’s mother that He would return Musa to her and make him a messenger.
  • This was very difficult for Musa’s mom, as it would be for any mother, because she loved him so much and newborn babies are so dependent on their moms. She was so afraid for his safety.
  • Allah SWT strengthened her heart, though, so she was able to do what Allah SWT told her — she nursed the baby and then put him in a basket to float on the river.
  • Musa’s mother had her daughter, Musa’s older sister, keep an eye on Musa AS and where the basket would go. His sister played an important role in following the baby without anyone suspecting her since she was a young girl.
  • Musa AS made it all the way to the palace of Fir’awn! Aasiya AS, the wife of Fir’awn, was a very good woman (even though her husband was a horrible person). When she saw Musa AS, the little baby in the basket, her heart was filled with love for him. She asked Fir’awn if they could keep the baby, and Allah allowed her to convince him. Thus Fir’awn agreed to keep the baby in the palace.
  • However, Allah caused a very special miracle to happen to comfort Musa’s mom. Baby Musa AS refused to nurse from any woman! His sister who witnessed this told them that she knew of someone who may be able to feed him.
  • Remember, she was undercover this whole time and no one suspected her since she was a young girl. When his sister saw that baby Musa would not nurse from any other woman, she told them she knew of someone who may be able to nurse the baby. They were desperate at this point to find someone to feed baby Musa AS and asked her to bring whoever she knew.
  • This was the golden opportunity Allah had created for Musa’s mother to be reunited with him again, just as Allah had promised her. Undercover (remember they didn’t know she was his mother), Musa’s mother was able to nurse Musa and be comforted by his safety, taking care of her own son in Fir’awn’s palace!
  • In this way, Allah allowed baby Musa to grow up in Fir’awn’s palace.
Takeaways
  • Allah SWT asked Musa’s mother to do something that felt scary and impossible but He also made her a beautiful promise. Whenever we obey Allah, it’s followed by beautiful rewards in this dunya and the akhirah.
  • The river didn’t just carry water — it carried a baby prophet to safety. Allah used water as a means of protection and mercy.
  • Musa’s sister showed courage by watching and acting at just the right moment. Sometimes we are part of how Allah’s plan unfolds for someone else.

For Kids 1–4: Floating Boats
Materials to bring from home
  • Pieces of paper and foil
Materials to collect from nature
  • Leaves of different sizes
Activity
  1. Find a puddle, stream edge, or calm water spot.
  2. Build a simple boat using a large leaf. Fold the sides slightly.
  3. Also build boats from paper or foil.
  4. Load your boat with a tiny natural ‘cargo’: a pebble, a seed, a twig, or a clod of dirt.
  5. Set it in the water and watch.
    • Does it float?
    • Does the current carry it somewhere?
    • In which direction is the water flowing?
  6. Reflect: seeds do this on their own, no hands needed. Allah designed them for exactly this journey.

For Kids 5–8: Build A River
Materials to bring from home
  • 1 large disposable aluminum foil tray
  • A watering can, pitcher, or water bottle
  • Scissors
Materials to collect from nature
  • Dirt or sand
  • Different sized rocks
  • Sticks
Activity
  1. Take your aluminum pan and cut open one of the short edges. Fold that flap down flat so that the end of the pan is open — this is where the water and sediment will flow out.
  2. Prop up the opposite (closed) end on a book or rock, about 4–6 inches off the ground. The open end sits low. Now you have a sloped landscape with an exit point where you can watch the dirt and sand spill out as the water flows through.
  3. Pile dirt and sand onto your slanted tray surface to create hills and a valley running downhill.
  4. Press some rocks into the dirt to act as boulders — scatter them across the slope.
  5. Stick a few twigs in as trees along the banks.
  6. Slowly pour water from the high end and watch what happens. Where does the water go first? Does it carve a path? Does it bend around rocks?
  7. Try pouring faster. Does the river change?

Resource: Alternate way to build a river →

Discussion

Where did the dirt end up by the end?

Some of the dirt, rocks, and sticks likely flowed down with the water in your river.

Did your river flow straight or did it start to curve? Why are rivers windy?

Rivers are windy because the land they flow over isn’t perfectly flat. Once there is a little bend in the direction of a river, that bend grows and grows. The water going around the bend gets pushed to the outside, and little by little, it makes the bend bigger through erosion.

What would happen if you removed all the rocks and sticks?

The water would flow faster and carry more dirt with it. Rocks and roots slow water down and hold soil in place. Without them, erosion happens much faster — which is exactly what we see in areas where trees have been cleared or riverbanks have been disturbed.

What would happen if you made your river less or more steep? Try it out.

What would happen if you pour water faster or slower?

For Kids 9–12: What Makes the Best Dam?
Materials to bring from home
  • 3 disposable aluminum foil trays
  • A watering can, pitcher, or water bottle
  • Scissors
  • 10–15 popsicle sticks
Materials to collect from nature
  • A handful of small and medium rocks
  • Sticks and twigs
  • Optional: mud, leaves, grass clumps
Activity
  1. Take your aluminum pans and cut open one of the short edges for each. Fold that flap down flat so that the end of the pan is open — this is where the water and sediment will flow out.
  2. Set up your pan on a slope — open end low, closed end propped up 4–6 inches.
  3. Add a layer of sand or dirt across the whole pan, and carve a channel down the slope to create a riverbed.
  4. Mark a line across the middle of the pan with a stick — this is where all three sets will be compared.
    • Set 1 — Control (no dam): Pour one full measuring cup of water from the high end. Watch how far and fast it flows out the open end. This is your baseline — no dam, nothing stopping it.
    • Set 2 — Rock dam: Pack rocks tightly across the marked line, same width and height as you can manage. Pour the same amount of water from the same spot. How much gets through? How fast?
    • Set 3 — Popsicle stick dam: Pack popsicle sticks tightly across the same line, same size as the rock dam. Pour the same amount of water from the same spot again. How much gets through this time?
Discussion

What materials worked to create the best dam to stop the flow of water?

Why did we keep everything the same except the dam material?

That’s called controlling your variables. If you changed the slope, the amount of water, or the size of the dam between sets, you wouldn’t know which thing caused the difference. Keeping everything equal means the only thing being tested is the material itself.

What would make your dam even better?

Adding mud or leaves to fill the gaps between rocks or sticks. Beavers figured this out on their own — they pack mud between branches to make an almost watertight seal. Engineers call this grouting.

What are some reasons people build dams?

Dams serve a surprising number of purposes — controlling water supply for people and livestock, generating electricity, preventing floods, containing pollution, and even creating lakes for recreation.

Do any animals build dams?

Yes — beavers are the only animals that build them. Muskrats and otters will sometimes move into a beaver dam, but they don’t build their own.

Prophetic Wisdom

Find an opportunity to share the wisdom of our Prophet ﷺ as you walk.

بَيْنَمَا رَجُلٌ يَمْشِي بِطَرِيقٍ اشْتَدَّ عَلَيْهِ الْعَطَشُ، فَوَجَدَ بِئْرًا فَنَزَلَ فِيهَا فَشَرِبَ ثُمَّ خَرَجَ، فَإِذَا كَلْبٌ يَلْهَثُ يَأْكُلُ الثَّرَى مِنَ الْعَطَشِ فَقَالَ الرَّجُلُ لَقَدْ بَلَغَ هَذَا الْكَلْبَ مِنَ الْعَطَشِ مِثْلُ الَّذِي كَانَ بَلَغَ بِي، فَنَزَلَ الْبِئْرَ فَمَلأَ خُفَّهُ، ثُمَّ أَمْسَكَهُ بِفِيهِ، فَسَقَى الْكَلْبَ، فَشَكَرَ اللَّهُ لَهُ فَغَفَرَ لَهُ ‏"‏‏.‏ قَالُوا يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ وَإِنَّ لَنَا فِي الْبَهَائِمِ أَجْرًا‏.‏ فَقَالَ ‏"‏ فِي كُلِّ ذَاتِ كَبِدٍ رَطْبَةٍ أَجْرٌ ‏"‏‏

“While a man was walking on a road, he became very thirsty. Then he came across a well, got down into it, drank (of its water) and then came out. Meanwhile, he saw a dog panting and licking mud due to his extreme thirst. The man said to himself ‘This dog is suffering from the same state of thirst as I did.’ So he went down the well (again) and filled his shoe (with water) and held it in his mouth and watered the dog. Allah thanked him for that deed and forgave him.” The people asked, “O Allah’s Messenger ﷺ! Is there a reward for us in serving the animals?” He said, “(Yes) There is a reward for serving any animate (living being).”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6009

This hadith highlights how our relationship with Allah’s creation should be as Khulafa Al-Ardh, stewards of this Earth. Last week’s hadith taught us of the great reward of serving people water. Here we learn that Allah rewards us for serving animals and His other creations as well. In this lesson we learned about how water moves. In this case it was stored in a well, maybe even long before the man or dog existed, yet it was there to serve their need for water. Think of how good Allah, Al-Barr, is to us that He prepares the water source for every single creation on this Earth before they even came into existence.

Additional Ayaat

The Criterion (25:53) ۞ وَهُوَ الَّذِي مَرَجَ الْبَحْرَيْنِ هَٰذَا عَذْبٌ فُرَاتٌ وَهَٰذَا مِلْحٌ أُجَاجٌ وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخًا وَحِجْرًا مَّحْجُورًا ٥٣

“It is He who released the two bodies of flowing water, one sweet and fresh and the other salty and bitter, and put an insurmountable barrier between them.”

The Most Merciful (55:19–21) مَرَجَ الْبَحْرَيْنِ يَلْتَقِيَانِ ١٩ بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخٌ لَّا يَبْغِيَانِ ٢٠ فَبِأَيِّ آلَاءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ ٢١

“He released the two bodies of [fresh and salt] water. They meet, yet there is a barrier between them they do not cross. Which, then, of your Lord’s blessings do you both deny?”

These are the two instances in which Allah mentions the barrier between fresh and salty water. This is cited as one of His blessings, as well as a clear sign of His creative power and His right to be worshiped alone!

The Cattle (6:6) أَلَمْ يَرَوْا كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّن قَرْنٍ مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مَا لَمْ نُمَكِّن لَّكُمْ وَأَرْسَلْنَا السَّمَاءَ عَلَيْهِم مِّدْرَارًا وَجَعَلْنَا الْأَنْهَارَ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهِمْ فَأَهْلَكْنَاهُم بِذُنُوبِهِمْ وَأَنشَأْنَا مِن بَعْدِهِمْ قَرْنًا آخَرِينَ ٦

“Do they not realize how many generations We destroyed before them? We established them in the earth more firmly than you, sent down abundant rain on them from the sky and made running rivers flow at their feet, yet We destroyed them for their misdeeds and raised other generations after them.”

This ayah highlights a very important concept: worldly success does not mean that one has gained Allah’s pleasure. Many see access to and control over natural resources as a sign that they are doing things right, and that Allah must be pleased with them, but this ayah proclaims otherwise.

The Light (24:39–40) وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَعْمَالُهُمْ كَسَرَابٍ بِقِيعَةٍ يَحْسَبُهُ الظَّمْآنُ مَاءً حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءَهُ لَمْ يَجِدْهُ شَيْئًا وَوَجَدَ اللَّهَ عِندَهُ فَوَفَّاهُ حِسَابَهُ ۗ وَاللَّهُ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ ٣٩ أَوْ كَظُلُمَاتٍ فِي بَحْرٍ لُّجِّيٍّ يَغْشَاهُ مَوْجٌ مِّن فَوْقِهِ مَوْجٌ مِّن فَوْقِهِ سَحَابٌ ۚ ظُلُمَاتٌ بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ إِذَا أَخْرَجَ يَدَهُ لَمْ يَكَدْ يَرَاهَا ۗ وَمَن لَّمْ يَجْعَلِ اللَّهُ لَهُ نُورًا فَمَا لَهُ مِن نُّورٍ ٤٠

“But the deeds of those who disbelieve are like a mirage in a desert: the thirsty person thinks there will be water but, when he gets there, he finds only Allah, who pays him his account in full — Allah is swift in reckoning. Or like shadows in a deep sea covered by wave upon wave, with clouds above — layer upon layer of darkness — if he holds out his hand, he is scarcely able to see it. The one to whom Allah gives no light has no light at all.”

The two places that true, absolute darkness can be experienced are in the deep sea and in underground caves. May Allah protect us from experiencing that level of spiritual darkness!

Yunus (10:22–23) هُوَ الَّذِي يُسَيِّرُكُمْ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ ۖ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا كُنتُمْ فِي الْفُلْكِ وَجَرَيْنَ بِهِم بِرِيحٍ طَيِّبَةٍ وَفَرِحُوا بِهَا جَاءَتْهَا رِيحٌ عَاصِفٌ وَجَاءَهُمُ الْمَوْجُ مِن كُلِّ مَكَانٍ وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ أُحِيطَ بِهِمْ ۙ دَعَوُا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ لَئِنْ أَنجَيْتَنَا مِنْ هَٰذِهِ لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الشَّاكِرِينَ ٢٢ فَلَمَّا أَنجَاهُمْ إِذَا هُمْ يَبْغُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ ۗ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنَّمَا بَغْيُكُمْ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِكُمْ ۖ مَّتَاعَ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۖ ثُمَّ إِلَيْنَا مَرْجِعُكُمْ فَنُنَبِّئُكُم بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ ٢٣

“It is He who enables you to travel on land and sea until, when you are sailing on ships and rejoicing in the favouring wind, a storm arrives: waves come at those on board from all sides and they feel there is no escape. Then they pray to Allah, professing sincere devotion to Him, ‘If You save us from this we shall be truly thankful.’ Yet no sooner does He save them than, back on land, they behave outrageously against all that is right. People! Your outrageous behaviour only works against yourselves. Take your little enjoyment in this present life; in the end you will return to Us and We shall confront you with everything you have done.”

This parable is one that is recurrent in the Quran: a depiction of a group of people, hopelessly lost at sea; completely desperate, they call upon Allah to help them, promising Him that they will turn away from their evil ways and turn to Him alone. Once He saves them and they are back on stable land, they go back to the way they were before. We must not get caught up by the specific details, and take the lesson of the parable in its entirety. Have we ever been through a difficult time, asked Allah desperately for help, made positive changes while we struggle, only to return to the way we were once the difficulty has passed? Do we turn to Allah in both ease and hardship, or do we only remember Him when all other options have run out?

Muhammad (47:15) مَّثَلُ الْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي وُعِدَ الْمُتَّقُونَ ۖ فِيهَا أَنْهَارٌ مِّن مَّاءٍ غَيْرِ آسِنٍ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِّن لَّبَنٍ لَّمْ يَتَغَيَّرْ طَعْمُهُ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِّنْ خَمْرٍ لَّذَّةٍ لِّلشَّارِبِينَ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِّنْ عَسَلٍ مُّصَفًّى ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ ۖ كَمَنْ هُوَ خَالِدٌ فِي النَّارِ وَسُقُوا مَاءً حَمِيمًا فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ ١٥

“Here is a picture of the Garden promised to the pious: rivers of water forever pure, rivers of milk forever fresh, rivers of wine, a delight for those who drink, rivers of honey clarified and pure, [all] flow in it; there they will find fruit of every kind; and they will find forgiveness from their Lord. How can this be compared to the fate of those stuck in the Fire, given boiling water to drink that tears their bowels?”

The rivers of jannah flow as our rivers in this life do, but they are so different in their contents! What kind of river would you like to drink from in jannah, inshaAllah?

Meaningful Movements

A flow of movements to get kids’ energy out at the beginning, or help them calm down at the end of the walk.

  • Begin seated, and reciting Zikr using Allah’s name “Al-Barr”. Close your eyes, sit cross legged and take slow cleansing breaths. Reach out with both arms extended, inhale and exhale, trying to touch the ground. Take another inhale and try to elongate the top of the crown of your head and with an exhale try to lean forward more, hinging at the hips, extending your arms and fingertips, touching the Earth. Imagine a stream of water flowing from your fingers, and with each inhale and exhale, try to extend the flow. Gradually draw the arms back, placing palms on the knees. 
  • Cross right ankle over left knee and take an inhale and twist your head to the right, place left elbow next to left knee, right arm extending behind you, with an inhale try to elongate and sit taller and with an exhale, try to twist deeper. Visualize the flow of water around crevices and creeks making their way around the river. With an inhale and slow exhale bring yourself back to neutral position, switch legs, crossing left ankle over right knee, inhale and twist to the left elbow behind the right knee, left arm extending behind you, similarly inhale elongating and spreading the river and exhale deeper towards the left into your twist. Visualize the flow of water and try to inhale and exhale at your own pace. Return back to neutral. 

  • Extend both the legs gradually in front of you, extend arms up overhead and hinge at the hips trying to grab a hold of your shins or ankles, take a few deep breaths and visualize the river ending up over a waterfall.

  • Return back to seated position, gradually lean head all the way back to flat, visualize yourself on the surface of an ocean floating, relax your arms, your legs, your feet. 

  • Recite the names of Allah and take slow inhales and exhales for as long as your body needs.

More Nature Activities

Here are more activities to try, whether as a group with your pod or individually at home.

For Kids 1–4: Rainfall Simulation
Materials to bring from home
  • 2 small watering cans
  • Water
Activity
  1. Find a spot outside where pavement sits right next to a patch of grass or dirt.
  2. Ask the kids: if we pour water on the sidewalk and on the grass, what do you think will happen?
  3. Pour one cup on the pavement and one on the grass at the same time.
  4. Watch together — point and narrate as it happens.
Discuss
  • “Look at the sidewalk — where is the water going? It’s sliding away! It can’t go in.”
  • “Now look at the grass — where did the water go? It disappeared! The ground drank it up!”
  • “Which one do you think the plants and bugs liked better?”
  • “The dirt is like a sponge — it holds the water and shares it with the roots underground.”
  • “The sidewalk is like a plate — water just slides right off.”
  • “Allah made the earth to soak up rain and keep it safe for plants and animals to drink later.”
Kids 5+: Salinity Experiment
Materials to bring from home
  • 2 clear cups or jars
  • Water (bring enough to fill both)
  • Salt (a generous amount)
  • 2 small eggs or similarly sized rocks
Activity
  1. Fill both cups with water. Add a large amount of salt to one cup and stir well.
  2. Gently place an egg (or rock) in the plain water. Observe — does it sink or float?
  3. Now place the same item in the salt water. What happens?
Discussion

What happened and why?

Salt water is denser than fresh water, so objects float more easily in it. That’s one reason the ocean feels different to swim in.

Extension

Try adding different amounts of salt to find the tipping point where the egg goes from sinking to floating.

Nature Stick Raft
Materials to bring from home
  • Rubber bands
  • Twine
Materials to collect from nature
  • 6–10 small straight sticks (similar length)
  • Long grass, flexible ferns, or thin vines to tie them together
Activity
  1. Lay your sticks side by side in a row to form a flat rectangle.
  2. Use long grass, fern fronds, rubber bands, or twine to wrap and tie the sticks together — wind the binding around and between sticks at both ends to hold them in place.
  3. Set your raft on the water and see if it floats.
  4. Optional: add a leaf sail by pushing a small stick through the middle of a leaf and pressing it into a gap between the raft sticks.
Discussion

How much can it carry?

Try loading your raft with a small pebble or twig “cargo” — how much can it carry before it sinks?

All the Water in the World

A visual demonstration of just how little of Earth’s water is actually available for us to drink — and why every drop matters.

Before you start, wonder together: If all the water on Earth fit into a gallon jug, how much of it do you think we could actually drink?

Materials to bring from home
  • A bottle of water (500 mL)
  • 4 clear cups or jars, labeled: “oceans & seas,” “ice caps & glaciers,” “groundwater,” “surface water & rivers”
  • Measuring cups and spoons
  • Optional: blue food coloring to make it visual
Activity
  1. Show the kids the full 500 mL water bottle. Tell them: this represents every drop of water on Earth — every ocean, river, raindrop, glacier, and underground aquifer combined.
  2. Pour the entire bottle into a measuring cup to confirm 500 mL. This is all of it.
  3. Measure out 15 mL and set it aside — this is all the fresh water on Earth. Pour the remaining 485 mL into the cup labeled “salt water / oceans.” Add a pinch of salt and stir. This is our oceans — far too salty to drink.
  4. Now take the 15 mL of fresh water and distribute it into the remaining three cups:
    • Ice caps & glaciers: 10.3 mL (68.7%) — frozen, inaccessible
    • Groundwater: 4.5 mL (30.1%) — underground, hard to reach
    • Lakes & rivers: 0.18 mL (1.2%) — this tiny amount is what most life on Earth depends on directly
  5. Hold up the lakes & rivers cup. That 0.18 mL — less than half a standard measuring spoon — is the water in every river, lake, stream, and wetland on the entire planet.
Discussion

Did the result surprise you?

Most kids expect far more drinkable water. Seeing less than half a teaspoon representing all the world’s rivers and lakes makes the scale undeniable.

Where does DFW’s drinking water come from?

From reservoirs — Lake Lewisville, Lake Ray Hubbard, Lake Grapevine — all fed by rain and rivers. That’s surface water, sitting in that tiny 0.18 mL fraction. It gets treated before it reaches your tap.

Why can’t we just use the ocean water?

The salt concentration is too high — drinking it actually pulls water out of your body’s cells and makes dehydration worse. Removing the salt (desalination) takes enormous amounts of energy.

What does this tell us about pollution in local waterways?

When oil, trash, or fertilizer enters a storm drain, it flows directly into that tiny fraction of usable water. There isn’t much margin for error.

What is Allah telling us when He made drinkable water this rare?

It’s a gift and a test. The Prophet ﷺ warned against wasting water even when washing for salah beside a flowing river. If a river wasn’t an excuse to waste, what does that mean for us today?
